Questions.

Which model should I start with?

On an 8 GB graphics card, Flux.2 Klein 4B in fp8 or an SDXL checkpoint. On 16 GB, Krea 2 Turbo, Z-Image Turbo or Flux.2 Klein 9B. On 24 GB, Qwen-Image in fp8 fits, and Flux.2 Dev runs in fp8 with 64 GB of system memory. On a Mac, count the unified memory: 16 GB runs SDXL, 18 GB Flux.2 Klein 4B.

What do I need to run these models locally?

ComfyUI, a graphics card or a Mac with Apple Silicon, and the model’s files: usually a diffusion model, one or more text encoders and a VAE. Each guide lists them with their sizes and the ComfyUI folder they go in.

What’s the difference between fp16, fp8 and GGUF files?

They are the same model stored at different precisions. fp16 or bf16 is the original, fp8 is about half the size with a small quality cost, and GGUF files (Q8_0 down to Q4) are smaller still and load through the ComfyUI-GGUF nodes. On a Mac, fp8 saves disk space but not memory, since it loads at full precision.

Do these models run on a Mac?

Most image models do, in ComfyUI on Apple Silicon, slower than on a comparable NVIDIA card. Pick full-precision or GGUF files rather than fp8. Video models are slow on a Mac and some samplers don’t work there; each guide says what to expect.

Can I use these models commercially?

It depends on the model. SDXL, Flux.2 Klein 4B, Z-Image, Chroma and Wan are open for commercial use. Flux.1 Dev, Flux.2 Dev, Klein 9B and Ideogram 4 are non-commercial, Krea 2 has a revenue limit, and HunyuanVideo 1.5 and MiniMax H3 exclude some regions. Each guide’s spec card names the licence.
