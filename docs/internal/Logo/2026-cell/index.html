Move over it

Built from the same cell as every render.

Every generation in HEISS starts as a grid of gray square cells that resolves into an image. The logo is built from that same cell. The wordmark is PP Neue Bit, one cell per font pixel, and the cup sits on the same grid.

Only the steam breaks the grid. Its cells shrink, cool and fade as they rise. There's one wisp above the I in the wordmark and two above the cup.

The cell

One unit, everywhere.

A 10-unit pitch with an 8.4-unit square and a 1.4 corner radius. The gap is what makes the mark read as a render rather than a font. Below about 24 px the gap closes and the solid variant takes over.

A solid body, a two-cell handle and a tapered foot. The steam is two wisps of six cells each, going from hot orange at the bottom to pale and small at the top. In motion it climbs one cell at a time.

Wordmark

HEISS, with a little heat.

The wordmark is the primary logo. The wisp above the I is its signature, so it stays even when the cup isn't there. In the lockup the cup brings the steam, so the I goes plain. "UI" is only used when naming the product.

Steam

Heat rises, cools, resolves.

Six steps. Each one up is smaller, cooler and more transparent. This ramp is the brand's only colour; everything else is ink on coal.

Colour

Coal, ink, ember.

Coal and ink come straight from the app's canvas and action colours. Ember is kept for heat: the steam, the active state, a single square.
